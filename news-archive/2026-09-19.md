### Trending News #1: ECB's Lagarde Accused of Blocking Binance License
- **Source**: Wall Street Journal / CoinDesk
- **Summary**: The Wall Street Journal reported that European Central Bank President Christine Lagarde personally intervened to stop Binance from winning a MiCA license in Greece, raising fresh questions about ECB overreach into crypto licensing decisions that legally belong to national regulators. Greece's Hellenic Capital Market Commission had reviewed Binance's application and was preparing to approve it as early as June, but a commission vice chair reportedly told Binance that Lagarde had asked Greek Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis not to approve it. Binance withdrew its Greek application in June and says it still intends to seek MiCA authorization elsewhere. Both the ECB and Greek authorities dispute the characterization. The episode threatens to reignite debate over political interference in the EU's supposedly harmonized crypto licensing regime, just as Binance seeks broader European market access.

### Trending News #2: CFTC Advances Crypto Rules After Clarity Act Stalls
- **Source**: CoinDesk / The Block
- **Summary**: The Commodity Futures Trading Commission submitted two crypto market-structure rules — "Regulation Crypto Asset Transactions" and "Regulation Crypto Asset Markets" — to the White House's OMB for review on September 15, signaling it will build a federal crypto derivatives framework on its own authority rather than wait for Congress. The move follows the Senate's 49-50 rejection of cloture on the bipartisan Digital Asset Market CLARITY Act, eleven votes short of the 60 needed to advance debate. The CFTC also issued no-action relief letting passive crypto wallet interfaces connect users to regulated derivatives markets without registering as brokers. The proposal still needs OMB clearance, a commission vote, and a public comment period before taking effect. For an industry left without comprehensive federal rules after CLARITY's collapse, the filing offers the clearest sign yet that regulators intend to act unilaterally rather than wait indefinitely for gridlocked legislation.

### Trending News #1: Haruko Cyberattack Hits 15 Crypto Clients
- **Source**: CoinDesk
- **Summary**: London-based crypto infrastructure provider Haruko confirmed a targeted cyberattack that compromised data for 15 institutional clients, with some client funds stolen. Attackers exploited a vulnerability in one of Haruko's processes to extract a user-access token, gaining read-only visibility into exchange API connections and transaction data, then used that access to siphon funds from at least some accounts. Client login credentials on their own systems were not directly compromised. Smaller hedge funds with weaker internal security controls appear to have been hit hardest. Haruko says it has patched the vulnerability and rotated its server-side secrets, and is now urging clients to add IP whitelisting for maximum protection. The breach underscores growing supply-chain risk in crypto trading infrastructure: as institutional capital increasingly relies on third-party connectivity tools, a single vendor compromise can cascade into losses across many otherwise well-run funds simultaneously.
- **Sentiment**: Bearish
- **Impact Level**: Medium
- **Link**: https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/09/18/crypto-tech-provider-haruko-hit-by-cyberattack-affecting-15-clients-some-funds-lost

- **Source**: U.S. Treasury (OFAC) / The Block
- **Summary**: The U.S. Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control sanctioned Iranian crypto exchange BitBank on September 17, escalating its crackdown on regime-linked digital-asset financing under Operation Economic Outcast. The action creates fresh compliance exposure for any offshore exchange, bank, or liquidity provider that has processed BitBank-linked flows, reinforcing pressure on crypto firms to tighten sanctions screening as regulators intensify enforcement against Iran's digital-asset evasion networks.

### Trending News #2: Visa Shuts Memecoin Credit Card Rewards Loophole
- **Source**: The Block / Crypto in America
- **Summary**: Visa has directed payment processors to stop letting consumers earn credit card rewards on memecoin purchases, closing a loophole exposed by a September 1 investigation from The Block. Crypto payment processor Crossmint, which powers memecoin buying inside the Fomo and Robinhood Wallet apps, had been coding transactions under merchant category 5815 — a digital-media code meant for streaming and audiobook purchases — letting buyers rack up ordinary points and cash back on crypto trades. Visa told processor Checkout.com directly to end the practice, after Chase had separately flagged the miscategorization internally. A grace period runs through next week, after which memecoin purchases must be processed as standard cryptocurrency transactions, which are typically excluded from card rewards programs. Mastercard has not disclosed similar action. The crackdown, which follows scrutiny from New York's Attorney General, removes a key incentive that had made credit-funded memecoin speculation artificially cheap for retail buyers.
