### Trending News #1: SK Hynix's Solidigm Weighs $150B Nasdaq IPO
- **Source**: Reuters / Investing.com
- **Summary**: SK Hynix's US-based memory unit Solidigm is exploring an IPO that could value the business at up to $150 billion, in what would become the largest-ever US semiconductor listing. Sources say Solidigm held "bake-off" pitch meetings this week with investment banks competing for lead roles, with a potential listing targeted as early as 2027 and proceeds that could reach $15 billion. The plans remain early-stage and could shift with market conditions. SK Hynix built Solidigm by folding in its $9 billion acquisition of Intel's NAND and SSD business, positioning the unit to capture surging AI-driven demand for enterprise storage hardware. A $150 billion valuation would dwarf recent chip debuts, including Arm's roughly $54 billion 2023 listing and Cerebras Systems' roughly $56 billion valuation earlier this year. If it proceeds, the IPO would underscore how AI infrastructure buildout is reshaping memory and storage economics, and could pressure rivals to accelerate their own AI-storage strategies.

### Trending News #2: Court Rules Against Kalshi in Ohio, Tennessee
- **Source**: CoinDesk / CNBC
- **Summary**: The Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals dealt prediction-market operator Kalshi a fresh legal setback, ruling that its sports-related event contracts are not "swaps" under federal commodities law and therefore remain subject to state gambling regulation. The panel affirmed a lower court's denial of Kalshi's injunction bid in Ohio and vacated a separate order that had blocked enforcement in Tennessee, clearing both states to enforce their sports-betting laws against the platform. The decision deepens a growing circuit split: the Third Circuit sided with Kalshi in New Jersey in April, while the Ninth Circuit backed state authority in Nevada in August. With federal appeals courts now split three ways, legal observers expect the question of who regulates sports prediction contracts — the CFTC or individual states — to ultimately land before the Supreme Court. The ruling adds regulatory uncertainty for Kalshi and similar platforms as they scale nationally, and could slow new state-by-state expansion.
- **Sentiment**: Bearish
- **Impact Level**: Medium
- **Link**: https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/09/25/another-appeals-court-rules-against-prediction-market-provider-kalshi-says-sports-contracts-are-subject-to-state-regulations

### Trending News #1: Bitget Hack Losses Balloon to $387.5 Million
- **Source**: PYMNTS / CoinDesk / Trending Topics
- **Summary**: Bitget has raised its estimate of losses from this week's exchange hack to $387.5 million, up from an initial $351.6 million, after further on-chain tracing uncovered additional stolen Zcash and TRON holdings, making it the largest crypto exchange breach of the year. CEO Gracy Chen has pointed to a North Korea-linked hacking group based on IP addresses and attack patterns consistent with past state-sponsored heists. Bitget has launched a Recovery Bounty Program offering 5% of any funds white-hat hackers help freeze, plus another 5% for funds directly recovered, and says several exchanges and blockchain projects have already frozen a portion of the stolen assets. Withdrawals remain paused while security checks continue, with Bitget targeting a restoration plan by 4:00 AM UTC today; deposits and trading stayed open throughout. The episode renews scrutiny of exchange custody practices and could push rivals to accelerate proof-of-reserves upgrades.
- **Sentiment**: Bearish
- **Impact Level**: Medium
- **Link**: https://www.pymnts.com/cryptocurrency/2026/bitget-suffers-years-largest-crypto-hack-as-losses-top-387-million/

### Trending News #2: Tether Downplays Exposure to Seized EQIBank
- **Source**: CoinDesk / Unchained / crypto.news
- **Summary**: Tether moved to contain fallout from a U.S. Justice Department asset seizure at offshore lender EQIBank, disclosing that its exposure totals less than 0.034% of its $18.775 billion reserve base, or roughly $64 million at most. U.S. prosecutors seized about $89 million, or 80%, of EQIBank's holdings at Wells Fargo and JPMorgan Chase, alleging its payment processor Capstone misrepresented its business to the banks; EQIBank now faces possible liquidation. Tether said it had no knowledge of the DOJ's case against Capstone and stressed the incident poses no threat to USDT's dollar peg or reserve backing, though the bank had previously handled USDT redemptions. The episode highlights lingering counterparty risk in stablecoin issuers' banking relationships even as the sector scales toward mainstream adoption, and lands as regulators finalize GENIUS Act reserve and disclosure rules for issuers like Tether.

- **Impact Level**: Medium
