### Trending News #1: Telix Buys Germany's ITM Isotope for $1.65B
- **Source**: Investing.com / Reuters
- **Summary**: Australia's Telix Pharmaceuticals agreed Sunday to acquire Munich-based ITM Isotope Technologies for $1.65 billion in upfront consideration, creating a vertically integrated radiopharmaceutical leader. Telix will pay $1.25 billion in its own shares and assume $302 million of ITM's net debt, plus up to $700 million more tied to regulatory approvals and sales milestones for ITM's lead candidate, ITM-11, which just completed Phase 3 trials for neuroendocrine tumors. ITM shareholders get 105.8 million Telix shares, leaving Telix holders with about 76.3% of the combined company. ITM supplies therapeutic isotopes like lutetium-177 and actinium-225 across 65+ countries, posting $273 million in 2025 revenue on a 40% CAGR since 2021. Combined 2026 revenue is projected above $1.3 billion. The deal closes by year-end, pending shareholder and regulatory sign-off, and cements Telix's control over its isotope supply chain as radiopharma competition intensifies.
- **Sentiment**: Bullish
- **Impact Level**: High

### Trending News #2: Jefferies Sees S&P 500 Hitting 9,000 by 2027
- **Source**: Investing.com
- **Summary**: Jefferies reaffirmed a bullish call late Sunday, projecting the S&P 500 will reach 9,000 by end-2027 after an 8,000 target for end-2026, betting on continued earnings strength rather than further valuation expansion. The firm forecasts $373 EPS in 2026 (35% growth, above consensus' 29%) and $450 EPS in 2027 (20.8% growth), applying a 20x multiple versus today's 21.5x forward multiple. Jefferies flags risks from Treasury yields, up over 60 basis points year-to-date, and rising fiscal deficits and tech debt issuance pressuring long rates — historically a drag on equity multiples. The firm stays overweight technology, financials, healthcare, and materials while cautious on communication services, consumer discretionary, and real estate, signaling Wall Street's earnings-led optimism remains intact heading into 2027.

### Trending News #1: SoftBank Launches $10B+ Bond Sale for OpenAI Deal
- **Source**: Reuters / Investing.com
- **Summary**: SoftBank Group launched more than $10 billion in dollar-denominated notes plus €1 billion in euro bonds to fund its next payment into OpenAI, a term sheet showed early Monday. The dollar tranche splits into 3.5-year, 5.5-year and 7.5-year maturities, while the euro portion covers 4-year and 6-year tenors. Proceeds will cover SoftBank's $10 billion installment for the third tranche of its OpenAI follow-on investment, due to close October 1, and will retire a bridge loan SoftBank had used to pre-fund the commitment. Citigroup and JPMorgan are lead bookrunners, with pricing expected September 24 and settlement September 29. The deal underscores how much debt SoftBank is taking on to stay OpenAI's largest external backer as AI capital needs balloon, raising leverage questions even as it signals continued conviction in the AI buildout heading into the notes' pricing window.

### Trending News #2: Bessent Pitches US-China AI Safety Pact
- **Source**: CNN Business
- **Summary**: Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng wrapped talks in New York Sunday, with the US proposing a new AI safety notification mechanism for Trump and Xi to weigh at Thursday's White House summit. The plan would create a US-China channel flagging AI incidents that rise to a national-security threshold, moving what Bessent called cross-border AI activity "from opaque to more transparency" between the world's top two AI powers. The proposal builds on a May discussion between Trump and Xi in Beijing that was never formalized. It arrives alongside broader trade talks covering tariffs, rare earths and a possible extension of the truce expiring November 10. Markets are watching the summit closely: a framework that eases AI and trade friction between the two economies would reduce a key overhang on tech and semiconductor stocks heading into year-end.

### Trending News #2: Paramount-WBD Settlement Splits State AGs
- **Source**: CNN Business / Deadline
- **Summary**: Paramount's $8.4 billion Warner Bros. Discovery acquisition hit a snag as settlement talks with a 12-state antitrust coalition splintered. California AG Rob Bonta, who leads the coalition, has pushed to finalize a deal, but New York AG Letitia James is resisting current terms over worker protections, with Connecticut and at least two other states also raising objections. Proposed terms include operating WBD's studio separately for a transition period, guaranteeing 30 theatrically released films annually with financial penalties for shortfalls, a binding pledge not to relocate Paramount's California operations, and a new third-party editorial adviser overseeing CNN and CBS News. A federal judge has ordered Paramount and the AGs to a two-day settlement conference October 14-15 in San Francisco. The split signals the merger, first sued over in July, still faces real regulatory risk even as Paramount pushes to close, leaving media and antitrust watchers focused on whether holdout states relent before the court date.

- **Impact Level**: Medium
