### Trending News #1: Kalshi Files for US Stock Perpetual Futures
- **Source**: Cointelegraph
- **Summary**: Prediction-market platform Kalshi filed with the SEC and CFTC on September 19 to launch perpetual futures contracts on individual U.S. stocks, intensifying the race to bring crypto-style leveraged trading to equities. The contracts carry no expiration date and use periodic funding payments between longs and shorts to track underlying share prices, structured as security futures cleared through Kalshi's own CFTC-registered clearinghouse, Kalshi Klear. The filing lands the same day Coinbase submitted its own single-stock perpetuals proposal covering roughly 50-60 names including Apple, Tesla, and Nvidia, while Kraken's Bitnomial Exchange filed for contracts on ten stocks targeting 24/5 trading. Kalshi already offers CFTC-approved perpetuals on Bitcoin, Ether, Solana, and XRP since May. The parallel filings follow the Senate's September 15 rejection of the CLARITY Act, pushing crypto-native platforms to pursue equity market access through existing derivatives rules rather than wait on stalled legislation.
- **Sentiment**: Bullish
- **Impact Level**: Medium
- **Link**: https://cointelegraph.com/news/kalshi-joins-coinbase-with-filing-for-us-stock-perpetual-futures

### Trending News #2: REX Launches 2x Leveraged ETF on Strive
- **Source**: Cointelegraph
- **Summary**: REX Shares and Tuttle Capital Management launched the T-REX 2X Long ASST Daily Target ETF (ticker: ASSX) on Cboe on September 19, offering traders 200% daily exposure to shares of Strive, a publicly traded Bitcoin treasury firm holding 25,000 BTC — the fifth-largest corporate bitcoin stash. The fund resets leverage daily, meaning returns over weeks or months can diverge sharply from simply doubling Strive's actual share performance, a risk common to all daily-leveraged products. Strive recently financed a 469-BTC purchase through preferred stock sales, deepening its treasury-accumulation strategy. REX and Tuttle already run similar 2x leveraged ETFs tracking Strategy, BitMine, Cipher Mining, Circle, and SharpLink, extending a wave of leveraged products built around bitcoin-holding public companies rather than bitcoin itself. The launch gives short-term traders a fresh vehicle to speculate on corporate bitcoin treasuries, but the daily-reset mechanics make it unsuitable for buy-and-hold investors.
- **Sentiment**: Neutral
- **Impact Level**: Low
- **Link**: https://cointelegraph.com/news/rex-launches-2x-leveraged-etf-tied-to-bitcoin-treasury-firm-strive

### Trending News #2: Analysts Name Winners of SEC Tokenized-Stock Push
- **Source**: CoinDesk
- **Summary**: Goldman Sachs and Citizens analysts published notes naming Coinbase, Robinhood, and Circle as likely early winners of the SEC's new five-year innovation exemption letting tokenized U.S. stocks trade via automated market makers on public blockchains. Coinbase is best positioned, analysts said, because its existing tokenized-stock brokerage product already satisfies most exemption requirements, including preserving shareholder dividend and voting rights. Robinhood's overseas stock tokens, by contrast, are derivatives offering only price exposure and will need reworking to qualify under the new framework. Circle stands to gain indirectly as USDC usage expands for onchain settlement and collateral. Goldman flagged new opportunities in custody, tokenization infrastructure, and stablecoin settlement, while cautioning the near-term earnings impact remains limited given the exemption's volume and stock-count caps. The report reinforces Coinbase as the structural favorite in Wall Street's push to move equities onchain, even as rivals race to adapt their own tokenized products.
- **Sentiment**: Bullish
- **Impact Level**: Medium
- **Link**: https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/09/20/coinbase-robinhood-circle-could-be-early-winners-of-sec-s-tokenized-stock-push-analysts-say

### Trending News #2: Fed's Kashkari: Inflation Still Too High Everywhere
- **Source**: Reuters / Investing.com
- **Summary**: Minneapolis Fed President Neel Kashkari said Sunday that inflation remains elevated "in all aspects of the economy," not just energy, pushing back on the idea rising prices are mainly an oil story. Kashkari backed last week's unanimous quarter-point hike to 3.75%-4.00%, having earlier dissented in favor of hiking even sooner. The Fed's own dot plot shows all but two policymakers expect at least one more quarter-point hike this year, with markets now pricing rates ending 2026 near 4.00%-4.25%. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh pegged August core inflation near 3.6%, noting "too many categories" are still running hot, while Middle East tensions — the Iran standoff and Saudi pipeline disruptions — keep crude prices elevated and complicate the inflation picture further. Kashkari added the Fed "has tools to bring that back down." The comments signal the tightening cycle isn't over, keeping pressure on rate-sensitive sectors like housing, tech, and small caps into year-end.
